## Problem 1: Closure Properties of Decidable Languages (30 pts)

### (a) Union (15 pts)
To show that the collection of decidable languages is closed under union:

1. Let $L_1$  and $L_2$  be decidable languages.
2. Since $L_1$  and $L_2$  are decidable, there exist Turing machines $M_1$  and $M_2$  that decide them, respectively.
3. Construct a new Turing machine $M$  that, given an input $w$ :
   - Simulates $M_1$  on $w$ . If $M_1$  accepts, $M$  accepts.
   - Simulates $M_2$  on $w$ . If $M_2$  accepts, $M$  accepts.
   - If both $M_1$  and $M_2$  reject, $M$  rejects.
4. Since $M_1$  and $M_2$  are deciders, $M$  halts on all inputs and decides $L_1 \cup L_2$ .

Thus, decidable languages are closed under union.

### (b) Intersection (15 pts)
To show that the collection of decidable languages is closed under intersection:

1. Let $L_1$  and $L_2$  be decidable languages.
2. Since $L_1$  and $L_2$  are decidable, there exist Turing machines $M_1$  and $M_2$  that decide them, respectively.
3. Construct a new Turing machine $M$  that, given an input $w$ :
   - Simulates $M_1$  on $w$ . If $M_1$  rejects, $M$  rejects.
   - Simulates $M_2$  on $w$ . If $M_2$  accepts, $M$  accepts.
   - If either $M_1$  or $M_2$  rejects, $M$  rejects.
4. Since $M_1$  and $M_2$  are deciders, $M$  halts on all inputs and decides $L_1 \cap L_2$ .

Thus, decidable languages are closed under intersection.

## Problem 2: Decidability of $A_\varepsilon^{CFG}$  (30 pts)

### Problem
Prove that $A_\varepsilon^{CFG} = \{\langle G \rangle \mid G \text{ is a CFG that generates } \varepsilon \}$  is decidable.

### Solution
1. Input: A context-free grammar $G$ .
2. Algorithm:
   - Eliminate unreachable productions from $G$ .
   - Check if the start variable of $G$  can derive $\varepsilon$  using the following steps:
     - Mark all variables that directly produce $\varepsilon$ .
     - Iteratively mark variables that produce $\varepsilon$  through marked variables.
   - If the start variable is marked, accept; otherwise, reject.
3. Since the above steps involve finite checks and operations on $G$ , the algorithm halts and decides $A_\varepsilon^{CFG}$ .

Thus, $A_\varepsilon^{CFG}$  is decidable.

## Problem 3: Decidability of $A$  (40 pts)

### (a) Prove $M$  accepts all strings in $(01)^* \iff L(M) \cap (01)^* = \emptyset$  (20 pts)

#### Forward Direction:
1. Assume $M$  accepts all strings in $(01)^*$ .
2. For any string $w \in (01)^*$ , $w \in L(M)$ .
3. Thus, $L(M) \cap (01)^* = (01)^* \neq \emptyset$ .

#### Reverse Direction:
1. Assume $L(M) \cap (01)^* = \emptyset$ .
2. For all $w \in (01)^*$ , $w \notin L(M)$ .
3. Therefore, $M$  does not accept all strings in $(01)^*$ .

Thus, the equivalence holds.

### (b) Construct a Decider $H$  (20 pts)

#### Decider Construction:
1. Input: $\langle M \rangle$ , where $M$  is a DFA.
2. Algorithm:
   - Construct a DFA $M'$  such that $L(M') = L(M) \cap (01)^*$ .
   - Use the decider for $EDFA$  (the language of DFAs with empty languages) to check if $L(M') = \emptyset$ .
   - If $L(M') = \emptyset$ , accept $\langle M \rangle$ ; otherwise, reject $\langle M \rangle$ .

#### Explanation:
- $M'$  ensures $L(M') = L(M) \cap (01)^*$ .
- Since $EDFA$  is decidable, $H$  halts and decides $A$ .

Thus, $A$  is decidable.
