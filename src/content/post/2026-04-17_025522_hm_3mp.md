Good morning everyone,
Let me start with the bigger picture.

We live in a world where software runs almost everything. 
For decades, human engineers wrote that code line by line. 
Today, AI is stepping in. 
But despite what the media says, 
AI is not magic—it is a powerful tool that still needs an architect.
This is why my topic matters to anyone who uses technology.

Today, I will briefly address three points: 
how my use of AI 
how AI is fundamentally changing software development, 
and what the true role of a human engineer is today.
First, how do I, as a Computer Science student, collaborate with AI?
While most people rely on simple web interfaces, my workflow is entirely different. 
I use command-line interface agents like gemini-cli, claude-code, and opencode. 
Instead of asking one model to do everything, I constantly test cutting-edge models and build "sub-agents" based on their different strengths. 

It’s like assembling a highly specialized army, where each AI unit works together to complete a complex mission. 

I even develop tools for them to interact with local environments—my own Model Context Protocol (MCP) project already has over 20,000 downloads on github.


Second, how does this change the way we build software?

Unlike translating a language where a human must carefully spot subtle errors, 
in computer science, the compiler is ruthless—it instantly points out AI's mistakes. 
Because of this, my workflow relies on Test-Driven Development, or TDD. 
Instead of roughly telling the AI what to build, I write the tests first to define the exact target.
Then, the AI writes the code to pass my tests.
A simple way to think about this: 
I build a race track with strict boundaries, 
and the AI is the race car driving to the finish line.

Finally, what is the core value of the human engineer in the AI era?

Because I have mastered deep collaboration with AI, I know its limits. 
AI can write functions incredibly fast, but it cannot design system architecture, nor can it understand why we are building a product. 
Our job is shifting from simply typing syntax to orchestrating complex systems and defining the rules.

To conclude, let’s return to the bigger picture.

AI allows us to generate code faster than ever before. 
But without scrict testing and human architecture, fast code just means fast bugs.

So, the future of software engineering is not AI replacing developers.

It is the human engineer acting as the commander, orchestrating an AI army to build reliable systems.
Thank you.
